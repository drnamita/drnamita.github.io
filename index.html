About

A career that keeps evolving.

My professional journey began in clinical medicine and gradually expanded into public health, education, research, and health data science. Each chapter has offered a different perspective on the same fundamental question: how can we better understand health and improve the lives of the people behind the numbers?

Clinical medicine taught me to listen carefully to individual patients. Public health broadened that view to communities and populations. Data science has added another layer — the ability to explore patterns, quantify uncertainty, build models, and turn complex information into evidence that can support better decisions.

Medicine gives me the questions. Data helps me explore them.

My current interests sit at the intersection of chronic disease epidemiology, cancer research, health equity, Bayesian and predictive modeling, public health informatics, GIS, and precision public health.

I am particularly interested in research that does not treat data as an end in itself, but as a tool for understanding people, populations, disparities, risk, and opportunities for intervention.

Beyond professional and academic work, I enjoy the quieter parts of life — gardening, cooking, sewing, learning new things, and creating things by hand. These activities provide a different kind of creativity and remind me that meaningful work and meaningful life do not have to exist in separate worlds.

Research

Questions first. Data second.

My research interests connect clinical knowledge, population health, statistical reasoning, and data science. I am particularly interested in research that can move from descriptive patterns toward meaningful explanations, prediction, and better public health decisions.

Current and recent research

My work includes population-based analysis, Bayesian modeling, cancer-related research, mental health, metabolic risk, health equity, and community health. Across projects, I am interested in how different characteristics and exposures interact and how analytic methods can help reveal clinically and socially meaningful patterns.

  • Breast cancer research: analysis of mental health outcomes, metabolic risk factors, treatment characteristics, and phenotypic patterns using All of Us Research Program data.
  • Bayesian diabetes modeling: Bayesian logistic regression using NHANES data to explore diabetes risk and compare Bayesian inference with survey-weighted frequentist approaches.
  • Community food access: assessment of food environments, household resources, and neighborhood-level access to healthy foods.
  • Health equity: interest in understanding how socioeconomic, demographic, environmental, and healthcare factors contribute to differences in health outcomes.

Teaching

Helping others learn.

Teaching and mentoring are an important part of my work. My goal is to make health data, statistics, research methods, and analytical thinking understandable, practical, and relevant to students in the health sciences.

Health sciences & data analysis

I enjoy helping students understand how data can answer real questions in healthcare and public health. Rather than focusing only on formulas or software, I emphasize understanding the question, choosing an appropriate method, interpreting results, and communicating findings.

  • Descriptive and inferential statistics
  • Research design and interpretation
  • Health data analysis
  • Evidence-based decision making

Mentoring researchers

Research mentoring is an opportunity to help students move from an idea to a clear research question, from raw data to analysis, and from statistical output to a meaningful interpretation.

  • Developing research questions
  • Organizing and understanding datasets
  • Selecting appropriate analytical approaches
  • Communicating results clearly

My teaching philosophy

You do not have to know everything when you begin. Stay curious, keep learning, and be willing to take a new direction. Sometimes the next skill you learn opens a door you never expected.
