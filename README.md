Research trajectory
Clinical care → Epidemiology → Population data → Advanced analytics → Phenotype discovery → Needs assessment → Intervention planning → Implementation → Evaluation → Population health impact

              My research has progressively expanded from individual patient care to population-level investigation and ultimately toward evidence-informed public health action. Clinical practice provided the foundation for understanding disease, treatment, and patient needs at the individual level. My subsequent transition into public health introduced a population perspective, including the use of community-based survey data to investigate food access, obesity, and social determinants of health.

This work was followed by the use of large-scale secondary datasets, including NHANES and the All of Us Research Program, to investigate health patterns across broader and more diverse populations. Within NHANES, I have developed experience with complex survey analysis and Bayesian regression, including the use of prior information to strengthen population-level inference. My All of Us research has expanded into mental health, metabolic health, cancer treatment, and machine-learning–based phenotypic clustering, moving from the study of individual risk factors toward identification of multidimensional patient subgroups.

Together, these experiences have shaped a research approach that moves from data to action: identifying population health burdens, characterizing priority populations, conducting needs assessments, informing intervention and grant planning, and ultimately supporting implementation and evaluation of evidence-based health interventions

This trajectory reflects my long-term goal of integrating clinical knowledge, epidemiology, data science, and public health methodology to generate actionable evidence for improving population health and reducing health disparities.
